# OBSCURA
**Optical Baseline Scattering & Continuous Urban Radiance Analysis**

OBSCURA is a computational framework designed to transition light pollution research from static, qualitative categorizations—such as the Bortle Scale—to dynamic, predictive atmospheric modeling. Artificial Light at Night (ALAN) is a rapidly accelerating environmental pollutant with profound ecological and astronomical consequences. However, current characterization methods are fundamentally incomplete. Top-down satellite imagery (VIIRS) captures raw urban emission but ignores how local atmospheres scatter that light back to the surface. Conversely, bottom-up field sensors (SQM/SQM-L) predominantly measure the zenith, ignoring the critical horizon glow that disrupts nocturnal ecosystems and migratory patterns. 

OBSCURA bridges this critical gap by calculating the specific atmospheric attenuation coefficient ($\alpha$) for any given geographic region. By integrating high-resolution Digital Elevation Models (DEM) with satellite radiance data and ground-based radial transects, OBSCURA mathematically models the spatial decay of urban light domes. The computational pipeline actively accounts for local topographic shielding—quantifying how massive geologic barriers isolate or reflect light—and utilizes Non-Linear Least Squares regression algorithms to determine exactly how regional aerosols, dust, and humidity scatter photons over distance. 

This repository currently houses the Phase I architecture: a highly reproducible, R-based pipeline that calibrates theoretical satellite data with hard field measurements to generate hyper-local predictive models. Future deployments will expand this infrastructure, introducing calibrated DSLR all-sky photometry to map three-dimensional light dome geometries (Phase II) and cross-referencing these spatial models with biological data to evaluate specific ecological and physiological impacts (Phase III). 

OBSCURA provides researchers, conservationists, and dark-sky advocates with the open-source tools necessary to autonomously predict true sky brightness across complex terrains. Ultimately, this repository establishes a foundational, physics-based infrastructure for the emerging field of Night Sky Ecology, ensuring that the global study of light pollution relies on empirically derived optical realities rather than generalized estimations. 

We happily invite collaboration to help build the definitive mathematical standard for characterizing the loss of the night. We have found every piece of input to guide our understanding and will be happy to recieve yours!
